IPyHOP integrated into the Mulder HTN

Same construction geometry, shear trajectory and physical executor. Three backends × six controllers × six profiles × two construction sizes. Seeds determine identical initial phases. Retained-minus-fresh is the same-engine comparison. Negative cost differences favor retention.

The synthetic transient profile rejects the first otherwise successful attachment at segment 10 once, after paying its real movement/time cost. This tests recovery software; it is not a calibrated biological disturbance. No graph roadblock or silk-resource model has been added to Mulder.

IPyHOP adds per-segment refinement tasks with finite current-target refresh alternatives. Fresh starts at the root; retained invokes upstream replan at the real failed/current leaf and replaces the full unexecuted suffix. Legacy retains its historical component-splicing policy and unpinned GTPyhop strategy, so cross-engine method counts are not directly comparable.

Planning wall time includes state adaptation and leaf mapping. Backend order rotates by seed; timing remains environment-dependent. Elapsed construction time excludes CPU planning time and includes the same configured repair delay. Physical costs are compared only for jointly successful pairs; computational costs include failures. Bootstrap intervals are pointwise, 2,000 resamples, not multiplicity-adjusted.

Key findings

Retained-tree recovery changes planning work, but does not improve physical outcomes over fresh IPyHOP in this sweep. All 720 same-engine pairs have identical completion, completed actions, repairs, elapsed construction time and travel.

There is a scoped improvement over legacy component repair: the configured-size filtered controller under 1 s sensor lag improves from 0/10 to 5/10 completion with either IPyHOP mode. A separate 10-trial control using the original GTPyhop domain with whole-suffix refresh also achieves 5/10, with identical physical outcomes and repair counts per seed. This gain is explained by refresh scope in these cases, not a unique benefit of retaining the IPyHOP tree. In the small lag profile, filtered completion instead decreases from 3/10 to 2/10.

For configured-size full control at 10 cm, 0.05 Hz, mean method calls decrease from 25,966 (fresh IPyHOP) to 25,532 (retained), about 1.67%. Physical costs are identical. Planning wall time excluding the recorded seed 0 is about 1.823 s versus 2.628 s: tree handling is slower despite fewer method calls. Timing is implementation/environment-specific.

All four retry-capable policies recover from the single synthetic miss, with either IPyHOP mode and with legacy repair. Endpoint execution remains unsuccessful in all declared cases. These are computational model results, not biological validation.
